Read of the session

Two reads, because at most hours of the day they disagree: the last cash session to finish, and what has happened since it closed. The second is built only from instruments still trading and re-reads itself every minute.

Futures

What the read above is built from, and the only thing on this page that keeps moving once the cash markets shut. Index futures are measured against the last close of the market they track, so the figure answers one question all day: where do we stand against the last time that market printed a close. Every card carries the age of its last tick.

Around the world

Cash equity indices, each in its own currency and against its own exchange's previous close — so Asia reads as Asia saw it. Amber is trading now, grey is shut with the time it next opens. Blocks run in session order, which is the order the day happens in.

Cross-asset

The cash reads that no future here can give: volatility, the yield itself, and credit. All are New York instruments, so they sit still outside the US session — amber while it is trading, grey with the next open when it is not.

Movers

Ranked by size of move against each instrument's own 60-day volatility, so a 2% day in something that never moves outranks a 4% day in something that always does. The σ figure is that ratio.

Breadth

Share of each block trading higher on the session — where participation is broad and where it is a handful of names.

On the calendar

What is coming up on the watchlist, nearest first, from the TradingView events feed. Anything due today or tomorrow is called out; the rest is listed with its distance so the section still tells you something on the many days when nothing reports.

Sessions

Every cash market this page tracks and whether it is trading right now. Open first, then the rest in the order they come back.

Market news

Headlines from Bloomberg's public markets and economics feeds, newest first. Titles and links only — the stories stay on Bloomberg.

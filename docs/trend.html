Fetching weekly and monthly bars…

The slow timeframes, and where the daily sits inside them. What this page asks is where something stands on the averages that take quarters to change their mind — the weekly and monthly — and then, in the alignment read, whether the daily agrees. Indices, country and sector funds, themes and commodity contracts; no individual companies, because a single name's trend is a different question from a market's. Yields are excluded too: the distance between a bond yield and its own average is a real number but not the same statement as a price's.

The alignment read

One sentence per instrument, from three timeframes at once. Daily 50 for the swing trader, the 30-week for the intermediate trend, the 10-month for the primary — Weinstein's stage line and the 200-day expressed in months, and fixed. The eight combinations are not eight equal boxes — they are the stages a trend passes through, and each has a name a trader would use and a thing they would do about it. Click a state to filter everything below to it.

Where everything sits

Each instrument placed by its distance from a weekly average across and a monthly one up. The two lengths are set separately because the axes ask different questions — the weekly carries the intermediate trend, the monthly the primary one — and the same number on both would pair a year against four years and call it a comparison. Change the lengths to look at the same instruments on different lines — a swing trader's 30-week, a position trader's 40, the 200-week cycle floor. This re-scales the map and the table only; the alignment read above is pinned to its own lines and does not move. The quadrant a thing lands in is the reading: agreement in the corners, disagreement in the off-diagonals, and the off-diagonals are where trends turn.

Major crosses

The crosses a chartist would mark on the chart, and nothing faster. On weekly bars: price through the 30-week stage line, through the 40-week, the 10-by-40 golden or death cross, and the 200-week cycle floor. On monthly: price through the 10-month tactical line and the 20-month, and the 10-by-20. Only crosses that have held are shown — one that was given back inside the window is chop, not a change of trend — and the window is short, the last four weeks and three months, so this reads as what just changed rather than a year's history.

Every instrument

Prices from Yahoo Finance, fetched live on each load and delayed rather than real-time, each instrument in its own quoted currency and never converted. Weekly bars come from a five-year window and monthly from twenty-five, which is what the feed will serve at those intervals — so a 200-period monthly average needs about seventeen years of history and the newer funds simply do not have one. Coverage is stated rather than assumed, and anything without enough history reads as a dash instead of being quietly dropped. Averages are simple, computed on closes. Part of Macro Compass.

Structure

Commodities are cycle assets, so the monthly averages carry more here than anywhere else on the dashboard — gold's monthly 200 has turned a handful of times in twenty years. Position against the 50 and 200 on each timeframe, and anything that has just changed sides.

Ratios that carry information

Cross-commodity ratios strip out the common move — the dollar, the cycle — and leave the relative story.

The equity leg

Miners and producers are a levered claim on the commodity, carrying operating costs, reserves and management on top of the price. Each is set against the commodity it tracks over a year: leading it says the market is pricing the move as durable, lagging it says the commodity is being held as a hedge rather than a trend. These names moved here from Themes, where they sat beside secular narratives they have little to do with.

Positioning

CFTC Commitments of Traders, weekly and reported with a lag. Commercials are producers and merchants hedging physical exposure, so in most commodities they sit structurally short and the raw sign says little — the percentile against five years is the signal. Large speculators are managed money, who follow trend, so their extremes mark crowding rather than insight.

Carry — what the curve costs you

A futures index cannot hold the physical, so it rolls, and the shape of the curve becomes a cost or a credit. Comparing a rolling ETF against the front-month price over a year isolates that drag. Gold is the control: its ETF holds bullion, so the gap should be little more than the fee.

Leaders and laggards

Country equity performance over the selected window, against two yardsticks: the all-country world index (ACWI) and the US. They answer different questions — whether a country beat the world, and whether it beat the alternative most portfolios actually hold — and since ACWI is roughly two-thirds US, they only part company when the US is the outlier. Every country is measured through its US-listed ETF, so all of these are returns to a dollar investor; the currency move is part of the number, which is what makes them comparable.

Window

Signal scan — moving-average crosses

Countries that crossed a key average in the last ten sessions. Columns run left to right along the escalation ladder — the leftmost signals fire first and fail often, the rightmost arrive weeks later and rarely lie. A cross that reverses again inside the window is flagged as whipsawed rather than hidden.

Structure

Where each market sits against its own 50 and 200 on the daily, weekly and monthly, and which have just changed sides. A country reclaiming its weekly 200 is a different event from one poking above a daily average for a session. Built on closes, so a wick through a level that closed back above it reads as held.

All countries

Forty-six country markets plus three benchmarks. Sort on any column header, and filter by market name or continent from the header row — the filter carries across the table, the charts and the yearly grid. Table and charts run on the US-listed ETF; the yearly view can switch to each country's own local index, or to excess return over the US.

Prices from Yahoo Finance, fetched live on each load (delayed, not real-time). Country returns are price returns and exclude dividends. ETF figures are in US dollars, so they combine the local market move with the currency move; local index figures are in local currency and strip the currency out — the gap between the two columns is roughly the FX effect.

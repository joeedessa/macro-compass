How frothy, and on what evidence

Measurable conditions, each shown with the raw number behind it and the mapping that turned it into a score. The composite is an average, not a model — it is here to be argued with, which is why every input is visible. A high reading says the conditions that have accompanied late-cycle equity markets are present now. It says nothing about timing, and these conditions can persist for years.

The funding gap

The question underneath everything else on this page: are the people building this paying for it out of what they earn? Trailing capital spending against trailing operating cash flow, then the same against what the sell side expects each name to spend over the next twelve months. Where forward capex exceeds cash flow, the difference has to come from the balance sheet or the bond market — which is exactly what has been happening.

Credit — where it breaks first

Equity prices the upside; credit prices survival. In every comparable build-out, credit moved before the equity did. Three views: the long history, the rating ladder, and what the builders themselves are paying to borrow.

The long view

Moody's Baa against the 10-year Treasury — a daily corporate spread running back to 1986, and the only credit series here with enough history to mean anything against previous cycles.

The rating ladder

ICE BofA option-adjusted spreads by rating. What matters is not any one line but the shape: an index at its tights while the bottom of the ladder gaps wider is a market that has already started discriminating.

What long-dated paper costs

Investment grade spreads cut by maturity. This one needs no history at all — the shape across the buckets is a cross-section, readable today. It matters because the hyperscalers have not been issuing at the front end: Meta's October deal ran out to forty years, so the long buckets are nearer to what these companies actually pay than the index is.

What protection costs, across markets

A credit spread and an equity volatility index are the same kind of thing — what somebody charges to carry a risk. Reading them together is the point, but a ratio between them would not survive its own units, and a ratio can only carry as much history as its shorter leg. So each gauge is ranked against its own longest record and set side by side, and the only difference taken is between the two that share a scale.

What the builders pay

The blended cost of debt each company is actually carrying, from interest expense in the filings, set beside its leverage and its ability to cover interest out of earnings after capital spending. That last column is the one that matters in a build-out: a company whose EBITDA less capex will not cover its interest bill is funding the gap with new money.

This is not a new-issue spread. The effective rate is interest expense over average debt, so it reflects the coupon stack a company has accumulated — Amazon's number is largely a memory of 2020 issuance, not a quote for this morning. It is comparable across issuers at a point in time, and it drifts upward as cheap legacy paper matures into a more expensive market. The gap to the 10-year Treasury is shown as context, not as a credit spread.

The circular economy

The defining feature of this cycle, and the one no data feed carries. Suppliers investing in their own customers, customers paid in supplier equity to place orders, cloud providers funding the tenants who rent their capacity. None of it is improper and all of it is disclosed — but it means a portion of the revenue growth in the tables above is capital travelling in a circle, and it is the mechanism that turned the 2000 telecom build-out into a credit event.

Accounting to keep an eye on

Concentration

How much of the market is this trade. When index gains depend on a handful of names, an idiosyncratic shock to those names transmits to the whole index and to every passive flow tracking it — which is the reason this page exists rather than sitting inside Themes.

Asia — the physical layer

Korea and Taiwan are where the AI trade stops being a narrative and becomes silicon. Memory is the part of the chain that has been genuinely supply-constrained, which makes the Korean market the cleanest read on whether the build is real — and has turned the KOSPI into something closer to a memory-cycle instrument than a country index.

The power constraint

Compute is bounded by electricity, and electricity is bounded by turbines, transformers, cooling and grid interconnection — none of which can be ordered on a twelve-month lead time. Which layer of the complex is leading tells you what the market is pricing: chips and the speculative tail leading is a narrative bid, power and cooling leading is a bid on the build actually happening.

Speculative temperature

How much of the bid is borrowed, and how far down the quality ladder it has reached. The profitless tail leads on the way up and stops confirming first — a rising complex whose junk end has already rolled over is the shape that has preceded previous turns.

Structure

Where the complex sits against its own moving averages on the daily, weekly and monthly. In a crowded trade the monthly averages are the ones that matter: they are slow enough that a break is rare, and rare enough that a break is information.
